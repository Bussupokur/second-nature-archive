# Second Nature — Dark Mode
## How They Find Us

*Reading time: approximately 6 minutes*

---

The message arrived the way most of them did, which was to say without any real announcement — no chime built for urgency, nothing that distinguished it from the ordinary trickle of the network's daily business. It simply sat there, one line among several, until someone with reason to look closely enough noticed it wasn't like the others.

Petra found it first, on a morning cold enough that she'd kept the stove going since before light, the kettle just past its first low murmur when she sat down at the terminal with the particular unhurried patience of someone who had learned, over enough years, that nothing on this network improved by being read faster. She read it once. She read it again, slower, the way she read anything that had a person's actual grief sitting somewhere underneath its careful wording. Then she got up, put the kettle back on properly, and went to find Dorian, because some things were better read together than reported afterward.

He was in the workshop when she found him, elbow-deep in the guts of a relay he'd been coaxing back to life for three days, and he didn't look up until she set the page down beside his hands, deliberately, the way she always did when she wanted him to stop what he was doing without her having to ask him to.

He wiped his hands. He read it. He read it again, which she recognized, watching him, as him buying time rather than needing the second pass.

"Again," he said, finally, setting it back down with more care than the page strictly required. "Where do we find these people. Or — no. Wrong question. Where do *they* find *us*. That's the one nobody's ever given me a real answer to, and I've been asking it for longer than feels comfortable to admit."

"You know roughly how it's supposed to work."

"I know roughly how it's supposed to work. Careful. Slow. A name passed hand to hand until it reaches someone who's actually earned the reaching." He turned the relay over once in his hands, not really looking at it anymore, just needing something to do with his fingers while the rest of him caught up. "This isn't that. This is a man alone somewhere, grieving, furious, finding us inside of a season. That's not the network being careful. That's the network being findable by anyone who wants it badly enough, and I don't love how often that's turned out to be true, lately, more than it used to."

"You said something close to that after the last one, too."

"I'll probably say it again after the next one. Doesn't make it less true for having been said before."

Petra didn't argue with that. She crossed to the workshop's one good window instead, the light through it thin and pale, the kind of morning light that made everything in the room look slightly further away than it actually was, and looked out at nothing in particular for a moment before she spoke again.

"You know who he is," Dorian said, behind her. "Or who his brother is, at least."

"I know exactly who his brother is."

She let that sit a moment, long enough that Dorian set the relay down entirely, giving her the whole of his attention the way he only ever did when he could hear, underneath a sentence, that there was more coming behind it.

"Joss came to us himself," she said. "A while before any of this. Before the thing he actually did. I was the one who talked to him."

"You never told me that."

"There wasn't a story in it worth telling, at the time. He wanted in. He had the right anger, the right vocabulary — all the shapes people learn to say when they want to be let somewhere they haven't earned yet." She turned from the window. "I didn't like him. Not for any one clean reason I could have written down and defended afterward. Just the particular feeling you get from someone who's already decided, in advance, which parts of himself he's willing to show you, and has gotten good at it."

"And you think the brother's cut from the same cloth."

"I think blood isn't destiny, and I'd be a fool to pretend it always is." She came back to the bench, picked the message up again, held it a moment without reading it. "But I also think a man who watched his own brother do what Joss did, and comes looking for us within a season of it, alone, out of a grief he hasn't finished having — that's not a shape I trust myself to read clearly enough to say yes to. Not yet. Maybe not ever. I could be wrong about him entirely. I've been wrong before, in both directions. I'm telling you what I actually believe. Not what I can prove."

Dorian nodded slowly — the particular nod of a man relieved to hear his own instinct confirmed by someone whose instincts he trusted more than his own.

"So we don't answer."

"We don't answer this one. Not today. Maybe not this year." She set the page down, properly this time, the way you set down something already decided rather than something still being weighed. "But I don't want you thinking that settles the actual thing you're upset about. Because it doesn't. The finding-us-too-easily problem isn't solved by turning away one man. It's still there, underneath, whoever the next one turns out to be."

"I know that."

"I don't think you do. Not all the way." She said it gently enough that it didn't land as an accusation. "I think you're using this one as a place to put a bigger worry that doesn't have anywhere else to go yet. Because the thing that should actually be keeping you up isn't a grieving stranger finding us too easily. It's the other one. The one who isn't only grieving — the one who might actually be capable of moving something, if he ever decides to move at all."

Something in Dorian's face shifted, a wariness settling into it that hadn't been sitting there a moment before. "You think he's going to."

"I think if he does, everything we've built slowly, for years, starts moving considerably faster than we've ever tested it moving. Resources. Reach. A name that opens doors we've never gotten near on our own." She let the kettle's distant, forgotten whistle rise a moment before continuing, neither of them moving to answer it. "And I think if he doesn't — if whatever's happening with Nadia and him settles into nothing, or he decides, in the end, that he wants his old world back badly enough to walk away from anything that threatens to actually end it rather than restore it — then none of this moves at all. We go back to being exactly as slow as we've always been. Probably for exactly as long."

"That's not a comfortable place to have our whole momentum sitting."

"No," Petra said. "It isn't." She finally rose to deal with the kettle, its whistle climbing past patience now, and paused at the workshop door with her back still to him. "This one, we don't answer. No explanation, nothing sent back — he just won't hear from us at all. The other, we don't get to choose the timing of. We just wait. And hope whatever's happening between them is happening in the direction that helps us. And try not to need it quite as badly as I think we both already do."

She left him there, in the thin morning light, the relay still open on the bench between his hands, and went to see about the tea, already wondering, quietly, whether they should answer after all.
