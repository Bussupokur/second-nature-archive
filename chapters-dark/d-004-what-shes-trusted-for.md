# Second Nature — Dark Mode
## What She's Trusted For

*Reading time: approximately 6 minutes*

---

Nadia Brackwell was splitting kindling when Dorian found her, which she took as its own kind of message — he could have waited for her to come in, and hadn't.

"You could have knocked," she said, not stopping.

"I did. You didn't hear it over the axe." Dorian stood at a comfortable distance, the way he always did with her, close enough to talk without making it feel like he'd cornered her at her own woodpile. "Petra wants something looked into. She asked me to bring it to you specifically."

"She usually does her own asking."

"Not this time."

Nadia set the axe down, which was answer enough that he didn't need to explain why that detail mattered. She wiped her palms on her trousers and waited, the particular stillness she used when she wanted someone to finish a thought before she committed any part of herself to responding to it.

"There's a man," Dorian said. "New to one of the western settlements, a few months now. His family's old money was the kind that would have made everyone on this planet comfortable, once, if it had ever been divided that way. His own is smaller, but real, and it didn't come from anywhere he was handed it — which is its own small mystery nobody's fully explained yet, and not the one I'm here about. He's been standing in a field most evenings for the better part of a month. Reading things nobody's identified yet. Not talking to anyone who isn't required to talk to him."

"That's not a profile. That's a mood."

"It's four weeks of the same mood, which is closer to a pattern than either of us usually gets handed this early." Dorian let that sit. "Petra wants to know what he's actually circling. She wants someone close enough to find out without being noticed being close."

"And she thought of me."

"She thought of you before she thought of the assignment, if I'm being honest about the order it happened in."

Nadia almost smiled at that, the way she did when she recognized flattery and decided to let it stand anyway because the alternative was pretending she hadn't heard it. "Tell me the part you're not saying."

"There isn't a hidden part. There's a part I don't have yet, which isn't the same thing." Dorian picked up a piece of the split wood, turning it over without any particular purpose, the way he handled things when he wanted his hands occupied while the rest of him worked. "His reading, whatever it is, has him standing in a field alone most nights instead of doing anything an angry newcomer usually does. That's the whole of what we actually know. Petra thinks it's worth finding the rest of it out before deciding what, if anything, we do with him."

"You keep saying 'him' like the shape of the question is settled. It isn't. Not until someone's actually stood close enough to know what he's for."

"That's exactly Petra's point, and exactly why she wants you doing the standing rather than anyone else available."

Nadia looked past him, toward the treeline at the edge of her own small holding, the kind of look that wasn't actually about anything out there. "I don't like recruiting people we haven't spoken a single word to yet. I've said this to both of you before, more than once, and both times it got treated as caution rather than as an actual position."

"It is caution. Caution's not nothing."

"It's not a plan either." She said it without heat, more tired than sharp. "You send someone to watch a stranger long enough, you start seeing a person instead of a shape, and then the watching stops being clean. I've done this work long enough to know which way that tends to go."

"Which is exactly why Petra wants you and not someone who hasn't done it long enough to know that." Dorian set the piece of wood back down on the pile, precisely, the way he did most things. "She's not asking you because she thinks you'll come back liking him. She's asking because she thinks you're one of the only people left in this who'd notice if you started to, and say so, instead of quietly letting it change what you report."

That landed somewhere Nadia didn't immediately have a response ready for. She crossed her arms, not defensively, just settling into the weight of actually considering it rather than continuing to hold it at a distance.

"Four weeks," she said. "And nobody's spoken to him at all."

"Not that we know of."

"That's not an answer."

"It's the honest one. I don't know everything he's done in four weeks. I know what's been reported, and what's been reported is a man standing in a field."

Nadia exhaled, something between a laugh and its opposite. "You're better at this than Petra is, you know. She'd have led with the money."

"I did lead with the money. You just weren't impressed by it."

"I wasn't impressed by the field, either, the first time you said it. I'm less unimpressed now." She picked the axe back up, not to use it, just to have something in her hands while she finished arriving at whatever she was arriving at. "How long."

"However long it takes to know something real instead of a pattern. Petra didn't put a number on it, and I'm not going to invent one just to make this feel more decided than it is."

"That's not comforting."

"It's not meant to be comforting. It's meant to be accurate."

Nadia looked at him for a long moment, the kind of look that had ended more than one argument between them over the years by simply outlasting whatever the argument had been about. "I'll go," she said. "I want it on record that I still think this is the wrong shape of decision, made for reasons I understand and don't fully trust. Not the wanting to know. The wanting to know from this close."

"Noted," Dorian said, in the tone that meant he would, in fact, remember it exactly this way, whatever came of it later.

"When."

"Whenever you're ready. Nobody's in a hurry except the part of this that's already been standing in a field for a month without us."

Nadia set the axe down again, properly this time, leaning it against the block rather than holding it. "Two days," she said. "I want to close things out here first. If I'm about to spend a long stretch being someone who wants nothing from anyone, I'd like to actually finish wanting a few things of my own before I start."

Dorian nodded, and didn't say anything else, which she'd long ago learned was the closest he came to gratitude that didn't want to announce itself as gratitude. He turned to go, then stopped, half-facing her again.

"For what it's worth," he said, "I don't think Petra's wrong to want this. I also don't think you're wrong to resent being the one asked. Both of those can be true at the same time. I don't know why that surprises people as often as it does."

"It doesn't surprise me," Nadia said. "It's just rarely said out loud by the person doing the asking."

Dorian left without answering that, which she took, correctly, as its own kind of answer.
