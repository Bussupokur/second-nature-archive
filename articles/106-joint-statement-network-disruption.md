# Second Nature
## H.A.R.D. Issues Joint Statement on Network Service Disruption (art. 4)

*Reading time: approximately 2 minutes*

---

The following statement was issued by the Harmonized Alliance for Rapid Deployment (H.A.R.D.), a consortium of the largest AI and web infrastructure firms, formed the previous year to coordinate industry response ahead of anticipated regulatory action. Issued on the fourth day of the disruption, this is the alliance's first statement in response to a live crisis. It was read aloud at a press briefing and distributed to broadcast affiliates. No written digital copy was circulated at time of issue.

---

We recognize the disruption of the past several days has caused significant hardship, and we want to address what we currently understand, and do not yet understand, as plainly as possible.

Preliminary analysis points to a severe geomagnetic event, consistent with a solar storm of historic magnitude, as the most probable cause of the cascading network failures experienced across affected regions. Solar activity of this intensity has occurred before, though rarely, and rarely at a scale affecting modern digital infrastructure this broadly. We are continuing to gather data and will share updates as our understanding improves.

We want to be direct about something several outlets have asked us this week: this event does not reflect a failure of design, negligence, or inadequate safeguards on the part of any single company or system. Where systemic vulnerabilities are eventually identified — and some may well be — they will reflect the reality that no infrastructure of this scale and interdependency has ever been tested against an event of this nature. This is a shared industry challenge, not the fault of any one architecture.

We understand questions have been raised regarding why electrical infrastructure remained largely unaffected while network and data services did not. Electrical grid hardening against geomagnetic disturbance has been a known engineering priority for decades, following well-documented historical precedent. Data infrastructure hardening against an event of this specific character has not previously been considered a comparable priority, for the simple reason that an event of this character has not previously occurred. We expect this distinction to be studied closely in the aftermath.

We ask for patience as restoration continues. We are working around the clock, in coordination with government partners across every affected region, and we will not speculate further beyond what current data supports.
